Asymmetry

Intelligence over goods and services

October 2026

Agents shop on infrastructure built for people. They either drive a browser, one screenshot at a time, or read search results that were never meant to support a purchase. Both are slow, expensive and often wrong.

Smarter models do not fix this. Better data does.

We take a shopping request, turn it into a structured product search, check a short list of candidates against their product pages, and return one product. Checkout goes through whichever protocol the merchant supports.

The problem

Browser agents take a screenshot, decide where to click, wait for the page, and repeat, often hundreds of times per purchase. Each screenshot costs 1,000–1,800 tokens (Anthropic). The agent sees only the 10–20 listings that fit on a screen, in the order the site chooses, and stops at pop-ups, captchas and login walls.

  • Across 16 agents, the best took 2.7–4.3× more steps than a person (OSWorld-Human).
  • On DeepShop, the agents Microsoft tested completed 11.6–32% of shopping tasks (Microsoft Research).
  • On WebMall, models working from screenshots completed 0–2 of 8 purchases. Given the page's structure instead, they reached 62.5% (WebMall).

Search APIs are faster but built for reading the web. Prices, stock and delivery times change by the hour, and an index lags behind. Search can describe a product but cannot buy it. And nobody stands behind the data, so the agent re-checks everything itself.

Results

Limitations: 10 tasks is a small sample, and it includes only easy and medium tasks. The paper's judge grades screenshots of an agent browsing; ours grades data from the chosen product's Amazon page, with the same prompt. Most of our latency is live Amazon lookups. We have not yet compared against browser agents, or measured merchant coverage.

Method

  1. Parse the request into a search term, numeric filters, a sort order and required attributes (one model call).
  2. Search the merchant catalog and apply the numeric filters in code.
  3. Shortlist two candidates from titles, prices and ratings (one model call).
  4. Fetch both product pages, check each requirement, and return one product (one model call).

The model only reads structured product data, never full web pages, which is where the token difference comes from.

Example

Request: “Wireless earbuds with noise cancelling under $80, 4.3+ stars and at least 1,000 reviews, cheapest first.”

{
  "product": "TOZO NC9 Hybrid Active Noise Cancelling Wireless Earbuds",
  "asin": "B0DD41G2NZ",
  "price": 27.99,
  "rating": 4.3,
  "ratings_total": 50428,
  "notes": "Tied on price with Soundcore P30i; chosen for more reviews and deeper ANC (45 dB)"
}

7.1k tokens, 4 product lookups, 34 s.

Checkout

For each merchant we try the available routes in order and fall back on failure: UCP (Shopify and Google), ACP (OpenAI and Stripe), a universal checkout provider for any product URL, and a browser as a last resort. Spend limits are enforced outside the agent, and the merchant receives a single-use payment token capped at the order total.

Where this goes

With complete, current data over goods and services, an agent could:

  • find the right product in under a second, instead of 10–40 minutes;
  • consider every listing for a need, not the first page of results;
  • match a person's taste, not only their filters;
  • know when to buy, from how a price is moving;
  • judge products by how satisfied buyers actually are, beyond star ratings;
  • handle returns, and buy with safety nets such as a cool-down window.
